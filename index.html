Overview

A practical, mobile-first walkthrough for resealing a Husco 5000 series sectional hydraulic control valve. Every torque and procedure here is taken from the official 5000 Valve Series Service Manual (SSL-2000, Rev 12/07 / VA-5000). If this guide and the manual ever disagree, follow the manual.

Typical applications

Used on ASV / Terex Posi-Track loaders (PT-50, PT-60 and similar) and other equipment. The valve itself is a general 5000-series sectional unit — identify your assembly (section count, spool type, end mechanism) before ordering parts. Name tags and casting IDs are in §4.2–4.4, pp. 57–59.

Two jobs, don’t mix them up

  • Field spool-seal replacement (§3.3, p. 28) — o-rings and wipers at each end of a spool. The valve stack usually stays together.
  • Complete reseal — also includes section-joint o-rings (and load-check poppet/spring if you are changing them). That requires unstacking the valve per the build sequence in §3.1, pp. 24–27.

End-cap style changes the extra seals you need. A mechanical spring-return cap uses the basic spool o-ring and wiper. A hydraulic-pilot cap (common on some loader valves, including the example RC30 list) also uses AS568A-019, AS568A-022 and backup ring 55047 — see Parts.

Prep

Safety, tools, parts and a clean, numbered plan before anything comes apart.

Safety — standard workshop practice

The official valve manual does not include a machine-level safety chapter. Treat the following as standard hydraulic practice, not Husco procedure:

  • Pressure and stored energy. Park on level ground, lower or mechanically block all implements, stop the engine, and relieve system pressure (including accumulators if fitted) before cracking a fitting. Hydraulic oil under pressure can inject into skin — a medical emergency.
  • Hot oil. Let the system cool. Wear eye protection, gloves and stay clear of spray paths.
  • Cleanliness. Dirt is the enemy of close-fitted spools (§3.2, p. 28). Clean the valve and surrounding area before you open it; cap hoses and ports.
  • Support the machine per the equipment manual. This guide does not cover loader-specific removal.

Disassembly

Number first. Then either pull spools for a field seal job, or unstack the valve if section o-rings or load-check parts are being replaced.

Spool handling — §3.2, p. 28

Clearance is very small. Do not bend a spool or raise material on its outside diameter.

  • Do not drop spools on hard surfaces.
  • If you clamp a spool in a vise, use brass, copper or aluminium soft jaws.
  • Husco collets for standard spool sizes are available for holding a spool outside the valve.

Seal replacement

Official §3.3, p. 28 — Spool Seal Field Replacements. Use the recommended method unless you have a reason to leave the spool in the bore.

Where a short summary differs from the manual

A common six-step summary leaves out three official recommended steps: removing the end mechanism from the cap end of the spool (D), reassembling that cap end with new seals per the end-mechanism instruction (G), and reinstalling the spool and end mechanism into the bore before installing the tang / clevis seal and wiper with the seal plate (H then I). This guide follows the manual’s lettered steps.

What changes

The alternate method leaves the spool in the bore. A steel rod through the tang / clevis holes stops the spool from turning while you work on the cap-end mechanism. There is no separate “number first / remove from bore” pair of steps in this method — if you have not already numbered parts, do that before anything can be swapped.

Do not over-torque the spool end

When installing a detent (and this is the only place the manual states the warning in words): torque the spool end to 7 ft-lbs. The spring will require approximately 25 lbs of force to compress. Exceeding 7 ft-lbs (9.5 N-m) on the spool end will cause spool deformation and result in spool bind.

§3.4 Installation of a Detent End Mechanism, steps 2 and 6, pp. 41–42. Same 7 ± 1 ft-lbs figure is marked on the spring-return and hydraulic-pilot drawings.

End mechanism — hydraulic pilot (solid spool)

Kit B12-108, 3-position spring-centered hydraulic remote, is the source of the extra cap seals on some loader valves (see the RC30 example on the Parts stage). A die-cast cap variant with the same seal sizes is on p. 36.

  • 55047 — backup ring, qty 1.

Testing / leak check

The official manual does not include a formal post-repair test procedure or a break-in cycle. What follows is a cautious leak and function check, tied back to the troubleshooting table in §2.

Not in manual

No pressure-test stand procedure, no “cycle N times,” and no relief reset sequence are given in SSL-2000 Rev 12/07. Use the machine service manual for system pressure checks and relief setting. Do not guess pressures or relief torque.

Example: 3-spool ASV RC30 reseal list (Matt’s)

Useful context for one ASV RC30 valve, not a universal official kit. The extra cap seals match the hydraulic-pilot end mechanism in §3.4, p. 35 (kit B12-108) — they are not in 51086.

  • 6× AS568-113 spool-end seals (official item 130, AS568A-113) — two per spool
  • 6× Husco 5029 wipers — two per spool
  • 6× AS568-019 cap-face o-rings — matches 41-019, two per hydraulic-pilot end mechanism
  • 3× AS568-022 spring-end cap o-rings — matches 5141, one per end mechanism
  • 3× Husco 55047 backup rings — one per end mechanism
  • 4× AS568-228 section-joint o-rings — official item 564, AS568A-228; four joints on a 3-section stack (inlet + two between sections + outlet)

If your caps are mechanical spring-return (B10-100), you do not need the 019 / 022 / 55047 parts. Confirm the end caps on the valve in front of you.
